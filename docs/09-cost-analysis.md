# 09 — Cost Analysis

**Caveat upfront**: per-minute AI/voice vendor pricing below is drawn from the vendor research in [02-voice-pipeline-and-telephony.md](02-voice-pipeline-and-telephony.md) (current as of July 2026) plus typical market rates for streaming STT/TTS components not covered in that research (Deepgram-class STT, Cartesia/ElevenLabs-class TTS). Infra costs are standard AWS list pricing at reasonable instance sizes. **Treat every number here as a planning estimate, not a quote** — the actual figures should be revisited once Phase 1 (see [11-roadmap-risks-future.md](11-roadmap-risks-future.md)) produces real measured cost-per-call data from the `voice_sessions.total_cost_usd` tracking built in [08-security-observability-reliability.md](08-security-observability-reliability.md) §2.2.

Assumption used throughout: **average call duration = 4 minutes** (typical for a qualification call — greeting, qualification, close). Adjust linearly if the pilot's real average differs.

## 2. Monthly cost by call volume

| 10,000    | 300,000        | ~$100,000–120,000      | ~$5,000–9,000 (larger-scale Fargate service (or a migrated EKS cluster if ADR-006's trigger has fired by this scale), larger Multi-AZ RDS + read replica, larger Redis cluster, higher S3/bandwidth) | **~$105,000–129,000** |

**Read this table directionally, not precisely** — the jump from 1,000 to 10,000 calls/day is exactly where the optimization strategies in §4 (especially self-hosting the voice SFU and aggressive model-tier routing) stop being "nice to have" and start being necessary to protect margin, since the AI/voice variable cost dominates the total at that scale and is the one line item still charged at a vendor's retail per-minute rate.

## 3. Cost per call vs. plan pricing (illustrative unit economics)

At ~$0.35/call all-in variable cost plus amortized infra, a platform charging tenants per-call or per-minute needs enough margin to cover both the variable cost _and_ the fixed infra baseline at low tenant counts (where infra cost per call is highest because it's spread over fewer calls). This is the standard SaaS "thin margin at low volume, healthy margin at scale" curve — worth setting tenant pricing expectations around explicitly rather than assuming linear unit economics from day one.

## 4. Cost optimization strategies

- **Self-host the LiveKit SFU at volume** ([02](02-voice-pipeline-and-telephony.md) §2): removes LiveKit Cloud's per-minute agent-session margin, replaced by owned compute cost only. This is, not coincidentally, one of the three explicit triggers for migrating off Fargate to EKS in [20-architecture-decision-records.md](20-architecture-decision-records.md) ADR-006 — self-hosting an SFU needs infrastructure patterns Fargate doesn't express cleanly. The crossover point where self-hosting is cheaper than Cloud is a straightforward calculation once real usage data exists — tracked as a Phase 3 decision, not assumed upfront.
- **Tiered LLM model routing**: route simple, high-confidence turns (field extraction, yes/no confirmations) to a cheap/fast model; reserve a stronger (and pricier) model only for ambiguous emergency classification or unusual conversational branches. This is the single highest-leverage lever on the LLM line item since most turns in a qualification call are simple by volume.
- **Prompt caching**: the platform base prompt + tenant/business config layers ([03](03-conversation-engine.md) §1) are static per call and cacheable — only the small dynamic runtime-context tail and the new turn need fresh processing, cutting effective input-token cost substantially on providers that support prompt caching discounts.
- **TTS provider choice and caching**: TTS is the single most expensive per-minute line item in §1. Using a mid-tier streaming TTS provider instead of a premium one is the biggest lever; additionally, **static phrases** (the greeting, the closing script template before variable interpolation, common recovery prompts) can be pre-synthesized and cached as audio rather than re-synthesized live every call.
- **STT cost control via VAD-gating**: only stream audio to the STT provider during detected speech (not silence/hold music/dead air), which most streaming STT billing already prices by audio-duration-with-speech rather than wall-clock connection time, but is worth explicitly verifying per vendor contract.
- **Right-sized autoscaling, not static over-provisioning**: the voice-orchestrator's custom-metric Application Auto Scaling policy ([01](01-architecture-overview.md) §9) means infra cost tracks actual concurrent-call load through the day (most home-service call volume has strong time-of-day patterns) rather than provisioning for peak 24/7.
- **Committed-use discounts**: once call volume is predictable (post-Phase 1), negotiate committed-use/reserved pricing with the telephony carrier and voice/AI vendors — the vendor comparison in [02](02-voice-pipeline-and-telephony.md) explicitly favors vendors (LiveKit, self-hostable) where this negotiation leverage exists, versus platforms with opaque enterprise-only pricing.
- **Per-tenant cost anomaly alerting** ([08](08-security-observability-reliability.md) §2.3): catches runaway costs (a bug causing retry storms, an unusually long call, a misconfigured tenant) before they compound over a billing period, rather than discovering them at month-end reconciliation.
